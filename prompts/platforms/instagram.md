# Instagram rules

These rules decide what the sharing phase makes for an Instagram channel (a business or creator account), after the article is published. Everything comes from that article: its keyword, focus keyword, title, description, headings, text and picture descriptions. Nothing is added that the article does not say.

## What goes out per article

- One image post per article, on each Instagram account the website shares on, when the account is set to post image posts.
- One reel per article, on each account set to post reels, as soon as the article's reel is made. Its words follow these same rules, written for the video.
- Sent the way the channel is connected: through its Buffer account, or through the Instagram API (by way of the Facebook page the account is linked to).
- Instagram does not make links in captions or comments clickable, so the post carries no link at all.

## The post's words

- Two to four short sentences. Start with a hook that makes people stop: a specific detail, a small surprise or a question about the dish or topic.
- Warm and friendly, like a person who made it, in plain everyday words, in the website's author voice.
- End by inviting people to save the post, for example "Save this for your next bake."
- Never a link, a web address or a domain name in the post, and never "link in bio".
- Three to five relevant hashtags at the very end, about the dish or topic.
- Never generic phrases such as "mouth-watering", "delve", "game-changer", "look no further", "you won't believe".
- Stay true to the article: no ingredient, claim or number it does not have.
- Alt text: what the post picture shows, for people who cannot see it.

## The post picture

- Made by Google Flow for Instagram: its own picture, not one of the article's pictures.
- Made portrait, 3:4 (896 x 1200), the closest Flow gets. Instagram takes a feed picture no taller than 4:5, so before it goes to Buffer the platform cuts a 4:5 JPEG copy from its middle (896 x 1120).
- No words, logos or watermarks on the picture.
- The scene comes from the article's own picture descriptions (the feature and close-up pictures).

## When it goes out

- After the article is published. A full Buffer queue makes the post wait about 12 hours and try again; it is never skipped.

## Limits

The platform checks every post against these numbers before it is sent. An answer outside them goes back to the AI with the exact problem to fix.

- text_chars: 80-600
- text_emoji: 0-3
- text_hashtags: 3-5
- alt_words: 8-20
