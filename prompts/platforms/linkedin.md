# LinkedIn rules

These rules decide what the sharing phase makes for a LinkedIn page or profile, after the article is published. Everything comes from that article: its keyword, focus keyword, title, description, headings, text and picture descriptions. Nothing is added that the article does not say.

## What goes out per article

- One image post per article, on each LinkedIn channel the website sends image posts to.
- Sent through the channel's Buffer account.
- The link to the article is never in the post. On a paid Buffer plan it is the first comment; on the free plan the post goes out with no link at all.

## The post's words

- Three to five short sentences. Open with one useful, specific point from the article, then what the reader gets from it.
- Clear and friendly, a little more polished than other platforms, in the website's author voice.
- End by pointing to the comment, for example "The full recipe is in the first comment."
- Two or three relevant hashtags at the very end.
- Never a link, a web address or a domain name in the post.
- Never generic phrases such as "mouth-watering", "delve", "game-changer", "look no further", "you won't believe".
- Stay true to the article: no ingredient, claim or number it does not have.
- Alt text: what the post picture shows, for people who cannot see it.

## The post picture

- Made by Google Flow: its own picture, not one of the article's pictures.
- Wide, 16:9.
- No words, logos or watermarks on the picture.
- The scene comes from the article's own picture descriptions (the feature and close-up pictures).

## When it goes out

- After the article is published. A full Buffer queue makes the post wait about 12 hours and try again; it is never skipped.

## Limits

The platform checks every post against these numbers before it is sent. An answer outside them goes back to the AI with the exact problem to fix.

- text_chars: 150-1200
- text_emoji: 0-2
- text_hashtags: 2-3
- alt_words: 8-20
