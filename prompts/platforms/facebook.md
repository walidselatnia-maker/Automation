# Facebook rules

These rules decide what the sharing phase makes for a Facebook channel (a page),
after the article is published. Everything comes from that article: its keyword,
focus keyword, title, description, headings, text and picture descriptions.
Nothing is added that the article does not say.

## What goes out per article

- One image post per article, on each Facebook page the website shares on, when
  the page is set to post image posts. (Reels come later.)
- Through the Facebook API, or through Buffer, as the channel was set up.
- The link to the article is never in the post. It is always the first comment:
  - through the Facebook API: posted as the first comment right after the post;
  - through Buffer on a paid plan: Buffer's first comment;
  - through Buffer on the free plan: there is no first comment, so the post goes
    out with no link at all.

## The post's words

- Two to four short sentences. Start with a hook that makes people stop: a
  specific detail, a small surprise or a question about the dish or topic.
- Warm and friendly, like a person who made it, in plain everyday words, in the
  website's author voice.
- End by pointing to the comment, for example "Full recipe in the first
  comment." or "The how-to is in the first comment."
- Never a link, a web address or a domain name in the post.
- No hashtags.
- Never generic phrases such as "mouth-watering", "delve", "game-changer",
  "look no further", "you won't believe".
- Stay true to the article: no ingredient, claim or number it does not have.

## The post picture

- Made by Google Flow for Facebook: its own picture, not one of the article's
  pictures.
- Portrait, 3:4 (896 x 1200), the closest Flow gets to Facebook's 4:5 feed size.
- No words, logos or watermarks on the picture.
- The scene comes from the article's own picture descriptions (the feature and
  close-up pictures), so it shows exactly what the article is about.

## When it goes out

- After the article is published. A full Buffer queue makes the post wait about
  12 hours and try again; it is never skipped.

## Limits

The platform checks every post against these numbers before it is sent. An
answer outside them goes back to the AI with the exact problem to fix.

- text_chars: 80-500
- emoji: 0-2
