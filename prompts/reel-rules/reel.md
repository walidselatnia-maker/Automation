# Reel rules

A 10-second vertical (9:16) reel made from the article, for ANY niche (food, pets, travel, DIY, home and garden, beauty, fitness, tech and more). It is made once and the same video is shared on every channel that takes reels: Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest, YouTube (as a Short) and TikTok. Viewers scroll fast: the first two seconds decide everything, and the video must work with the sound on or off.

## Read the niche first

You are given the website's niche and the article. The article is the source of truth: show only what it says or clearly implies. The niche decides the look, the actions and the sounds. Never bring one niche's habits into another (no kitchen sounds in a travel reel, no "finished dish" for a pet article).

What the shots usually are, by niche (examples, not a fixed list):
- Food and recipes: ingredients, preparation, cooking, the finished dish.
- Pets and animals: natural behaviour, play, care, the happy animal.
- Travel: the most striking view, local life, details, the destination at its best.
- DIY and crafts: materials, hands at work, key technique, the finished piece.
- Home and garden: before, the work, the change, the finished space or plant.
- Beauty and fashion: products, application or styling, details, the final look.
- Fitness and sport: the movement, correct technique, effort, the result.
- Tech and how-to: the problem, the key steps on the device, the working result.

## Timeline, second by second, with these exact time marks

The reel is exactly 10 seconds: five shots of 2 seconds each, a hard cut between each, a new camera angle at every cut. Every shot below says what is seen and what is heard, and nothing spills into the next shot.

- 0-2s: THE HOOK. Seen: the most tempting, surprising or beautiful moment the article supports, filling the frame (the result, the reveal, the key moment). Heard, from 0s to 2s: the voice says the hook line. It makes the viewer need to see more: a surprise, a promise or a curiosity gap that is true to the article. Never a greeting, never the title read out.
- 2-4s: STAGE ONE. Seen: the first meaningful stage, action or detail from the article. Heard: the real sound of what is seen; the voice may start its one middle line here.
- 4-6s: STAGE TWO. Seen: the next stage; the scene visibly moves forward, never a repeat. Heard: the middle line ends by 6s, over the real sound.
- 6-8s: STAGE THREE. Seen: a third distinct moment that builds to the result. Heard: only the real sound of the scene, no voice.
- 8-10s: THE CALL TO ACTION. Seen: back to the result, the reveal or the subject at its best, from a new angle. Heard, from 8s to 10s: the voice says the closing line.

The stages come from the article, in their real order. If the article has no steps, use three distinct moments or details it describes. Never invent a step, an event or a result.

## Every shot, in full detail

Before the first time mark, one short line sets what never changes: the subject (exact breed, person, object or dish, with colours and size), the place, the time of day and the main light. Then every time-marked shot describes, in this order:

- Layers with real depth: what is in the foreground (close to the lens, often a soft, out-of-focus edge), the midground (the subject and its action, sharp) and the background (the real place behind, with its own details).
- The camera: the lens (for example 24mm wide, 35mm, 50mm, 85mm portrait or 100mm macro), the angle (low at the subject's eye level, eye level, overhead, over the shoulder), the framing (extreme close-up, close-up, medium, wide) and the depth of field (shallow with a creamy blurred background, or deep with everything sharp). Phone camera, handheld when it suits the subject.
- The light: where it comes from (a window on the left, the low sun behind, an overhead lamp), where it falls and what it leaves in shadow, its quality (soft and diffused, or hard with crisp shadows) and its colour (warm golden, cool daylight, neutral).
- The style and the mood: a real, true-to-life look that fits the niche (bright and cheerful, cosy and warm, fresh and airy, calm and clean), with natural colours.
- The subject and its action, concretely: real people, animals, objects and places doing physically possible things, at fast real-life speed with quick, snappy motion. Write it for every moving thing (a dog's legs, a tail, ears, hands, water, steam, hair, leaves): "moves at fast real-life speed".
- The sound: the real sound of exactly what is seen (paws on wood, a bark, a whisk on a bowl, waves, a drill), and the voice when the timeline gives it one.

## Motion and look

- Every moving thing moves at fast real-life speed, energetic and quick, as a phone filmed it at normal speed. The video model tends to make motion too slow, so say the speed in every shot.
- Real people, animals and places; physically possible actions.
- The same subject, place, clothes and light in all five shots unless the article says it changes.
- Never slow motion, never a slow drifting camera, never slow zooms, floating objects, morphing or teleporting.

## Voice

- One warm, confident adult voice in English, the same voice the whole time, at a natural, lively pace.
- The hook line: at most 9 words, said from 0s to 2s.
- Between 2s and 6s, at most one short middle line that keeps the viewer watching.
- The closing line, word for word: "Full guide on {domain}." It is said from 8s to 10s.
- Real sound of the scene under the voice. Never music.

## Never

- Never on-screen text, captions, subtitles, logos, watermarks or app icons.
- Never a link or web address, spoken or shown, other than the website's name in the closing line.
- Never filler shots, stock-looking montage or claims the article does not make.
