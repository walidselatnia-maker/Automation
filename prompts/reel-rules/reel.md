# Reel rules

A 10-second vertical (9:16) reel made from the article. It is made once and the same video is shared on every channel that takes reels: Facebook, Instagram and Pinterest. Viewers there scroll fast: the first two seconds decide everything, and the video must work with the sound on or off.

## Structure, with these exact time marks

- 0-2s: THE HOOK. Show the finished result in full, at its most tempting, filling the frame (for a recipe: the finished dish, a hand cutting or lifting the first bite). At the same moment the voice says the hook line. The hook line makes the viewer need to see how it is done: a surprise, a promise or a curiosity gap that is true to the article (for example "This cake needs no oven, watch this"). Never a greeting, never the title read out, never "Hi guys".
- 2-4s: stage one of making it (for a recipe: the key ingredients going into the bowl or pan).
- 4-6s: stage two (for a recipe: mixing, shaping or the key technique of this dish).
- 6-8s: stage three (for a recipe: cooking or baking, the moment it changes: bubbling, browning, rising).
- 8-10s: back to the finished result, a bite or a pour, and the voice says the closing line.

Each stage is its own quick cut. The stages come from the article's own steps, in their real order; never invent a step.

## Motion and pace

- Natural real-time speed in every shot, energetic, real hands doing real work. Never slow motion, never slow drifting camera, never floating objects.
- Phone camera, close and handheld, a new angle at every cut. Real kitchen light.

## Voice

- One warm, confident adult voice in English, the same voice the whole time, speaking at a natural, lively pace.
- The hook line: at most 9 words. Then, over the stages, at most one short line that keeps the viewer watching (what makes this work). The closing line, word for word: "Full recipe linked below." (It fits every platform: the link is the first comment on Facebook and the pin's own link on Pinterest.)
- Real cooking sound under the voice. No music.

## Never

- No on-screen text, captions, logos or watermarks. No link or web address, spoken or shown.
