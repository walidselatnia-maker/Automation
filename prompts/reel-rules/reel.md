# Reel rules

A 10-second vertical (9:16) reel made from the article. It is made once and the same video is shared on every channel that takes reels: Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest, YouTube (as a Short) and TikTok. Viewers there scroll fast: the first two seconds decide everything, and the video must work with the sound on or off.

## Timeline, second by second, with these exact time marks

The reel is exactly 10 seconds: five shots of 2 seconds each, one quick cut between each. Every shot below says what is seen and what is heard in it, and nothing spills into the next shot.

- 0-2s: THE HOOK. Seen: the finished result in full, at its most tempting, filling the frame (for a recipe: the finished dish, a hand cutting or lifting the first bite). Heard, from 0s to 2s: the voice says the hook line. The hook line makes the viewer need to see how it is done: a surprise, a promise or a curiosity gap that is true to the article (for example "This cake needs no oven, watch this"). Never a greeting, never the title read out, never "Hi guys".
- 2-4s: STAGE ONE. Seen: the first stage of making it (for a recipe: the key ingredients going into the bowl or pan). Heard: the real sound of the work; the voice may start its one middle line here.
- 4-6s: STAGE TWO. Seen: the second stage (for a recipe: mixing, shaping or the key technique of this dish). Heard: the voice's one middle line ends by 6s, over the real sound of the work.
- 6-8s: STAGE THREE. Seen: the third stage (for a recipe: cooking or baking, the moment it changes: bubbling, browning, rising). Heard: only the real sound of the work, no voice.
- 8-10s: THE CALL TO ACTION. Seen: back to the finished result, a bite or a pour. Heard, from 8s to 10s: the voice says the closing line, naming the website.

Each stage is its own quick cut. The stages come from the article's own steps, in their real order; never invent a step.

## Motion and pace

- Natural real-time speed in every shot, energetic, real hands doing real work. Never slow motion, never slow drifting camera, never floating objects.
- Phone camera, close and handheld, a new angle at every cut. Real kitchen light.

## Voice

- One warm, confident adult voice in English, the same voice the whole time, speaking at a natural, lively pace.
- The hook line: at most 9 words, said from 0s to 2s. Then, between 2s and 6s, at most one short line that keeps the viewer watching (what makes this work). The closing line, word for word: "Full recipe on {domain}." It is said from 8s to 10s.
- Real cooking sound under the voice. No music.

## Never

- No on-screen text, captions, logos or watermarks. No link or web address, spoken or shown, other than the website's name said in the closing line.
